Touch version of pad_twin_first_slipway, in a 390x844 window: from a
resumed record, "resume" chosen and the first slipway, s4 to s3, built
with touches on the virtual pad's stick and A. 36 frames.

Twin: pad_twin_first_slipway. It carries pad_bankrupt's `initial_save`
byte for byte, as the twin does.

Layout at 390x844 (`love.window_layout` tall mode: pad rectangle at
(0, 486.75), scale 2.4375; `love.pad_layout.place`, half-open screen
rectangles), re-derived with LuaJIT over the two modules:

- A (292, 720): A is x 263.25-321.75, y 691.5-750.
- Back (160, 745): Back is x 131.625-190.125, y 715.875-774.375, inside
  the stick zone (x 0-195, y 486.75-779.25).
- Stick base (90, 640): in the stick zone, on no button; base 117
  (radius 58.5, dead zone 14.625). Pushed from it: (90, 580) Up (1),
  (90, 700) Down (2), (30, 640) Left (4), (130, 600) Up+Right (9), mask
  bits as `love.pad_stick` gives them.

Each finger has its own id, never reused here. Every frame starts with
`"pad"`, so the help-me-debug cut works as for the pad replays.

- f0-3: touch 1 on A, the revealing touch: it presses nothing (twin
  idle); f4 lifted. On the title A would choose the focused row: a leaked
  press there would start a new game instead of resuming.
- f9: touch 2 on the stick base, mask 0 (twin idle); f10: touch 2 at
  (90, 580), Up (twin Up), the focus on "resume"; f11 lifted.
- f12: touch 3 on A (twin A): resume. f13 lifted.
- f17: touch 4 on A (twin A): a slipway from the hovered s4. f18 lifted.
- f20: touch 5 on the stick base, mask 0 (twin idle); f21: touch 5 at
  (30, 640), Left (twin Left), aimed at s3; f22 lifted.
- f25: touch 6 on A (twin A): the slipway is built. f26 lifted, idle to
  f35.

Rules exercised: the reveal presses nothing; a stick touch's first frame
is mask 0; touches on A press A on a shown pad.

Negative scratch run: the twin with A on f0-3 (a leaked reveal) starts a
forgiving new game (difficulty forgiving, year 3401 month 1, $100, no
slipway) instead of resuming to year 3401 month 10, $37, slipway s4 s3.

Its `.json.sha256` and `.json.end-state` are byte copies of
pad_twin_first_slipway's, never a baseline of this run: `make e2e` and `make
end-state` thus prove this touch run equal to the gamepad run, frame by
frame and in the end state, and `tools/e2e_limits.sh` fails if the two
`.sha256` drift apart (after a `make e2e-baseline`, for example).

Not proven here: the drawing of the pad (step 16); a resize; overlay
mode; the 5 + 6 chord (covered by the Lua tests).
